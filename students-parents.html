Every big decision startswith a good conversation

Whatever stage you are at, speaking with the right people changes how a decision feels. Here is how to make the most of it.

What to focus on

Your stage setsthe questions

Early years

Explore

Think about what you enjoy and where you are strong. Ask how subjects link to careers, and what choices to keep open.

Middle years

Compare

Look at qualifications, entry requirements and costs side by side. Start a shortlist and note what each course needs.

Final year

Decide

Check deadlines, scholarships and foundation options. Ask what the next practical step is and who to speak to.

Ask the questions

Seven questionsworth asking

Admissions teams like specific questions. Write yours down beforehand, and note the answers so you can compare later.

  1. What are the entry requirements, and are there alternative routes in?
  2. What is the total cost for the whole course, not just the first year?
  3. Which scholarships or fee reductions are available, and when do they close?
  4. How many hours a week will I be in class, and how is the course assessed?
  5. What do graduates from this course go on to do?
  6. Is the qualification recognised where I may want to work or study next?
  7. What support is there for students who are far from home?
For parents

Be part of theconversation

  • Hear costs and timelines first-hand
  • Let your child lead. Your questions work best as a back-up
  • Ask what support there is for students living away from home
  • Take notes, then talk through the options at home
